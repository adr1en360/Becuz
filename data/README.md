# Data

All data here is **synthetic**. It was generated by `generator.py` from a fixed seed. No real shop, customer or bank data is used.

Each scenario covers 26 weeks (Monday 20 April to Sunday 18 October 2026) of one Nigerian mini-mart. Causes are planted only in the final week (12 to 18 October):

| Scenario | Planted causes | Red herring |
|---|---|---|
| `s01` | supplier cost rise, promo overrun, wastage spike, transfer leakage | POS charge rise (charges stay flat at 0.5%) |
| `s02` | supplier cost rise | POS charge rise |
| `s03` | promo overrun, wastage spike | POS charge rise |
| `s04` | transfer leakage | sales fell (they did not), POS charge rise |
| `s05` | none: a normal week | POS charge rise |

In every scenario, each weekday repeats the same customers, so only the planted causes change the final week. In every scenario, day-to-day spending repeats one weekly pattern, so running costs never hide or fake a planted cause. The legitimate promo runs one week in August, well before any baseline. The only other movement is a small drift from month length, because monthly costs are spread over 30 or 31 days.

Costs follow real rhythms, not one Monday lump:

- Rent, salaries and the LAWMA levy are paid once a month. Each row has `cover_start` and `cover_end` for the month it pays for, and `daily_pnl` spreads the amount evenly over those days.
- Diesel (20 litres, 2 to 4 times a week), transport (2 to 5 times a week) and a weekly electricity token are booked on the day they are paid, with the cover columns empty.
- Every product is invoiced on 20 April as opening stock. After that, perishables are delivered 2 to 3 times a week, fast movers 1 to 2 times, and slow movers once a fortnight, all on varying weekdays. A sale is costed at the latest invoice for its product on or before the sale day.
- `calendar.csv` lists every day from the first to the last day of data. It is the day spine for `daily_pnl`.

Regenerate a scenario:

```powershell
uv run python -m data.generator s01   # or s02 to s05
```

The same seed always produces byte-identical files. Each scenario folder holds:

- `pos`: `products`, `staff`, `sales`, `sale_lines`, `promos`, `stock_movements`
- `bank`: `bank_statement`
- `books`: `supplier_invoices`, `expense_book` (with `cover_start` and `cover_end`)
- `context`: `fx_rates`, `calendar`
- `answer_key.json`: what was planted and its naira impact, computed from the generator's own clean ledger. The eval reads this file; the agent never does.

Money is in naira with two decimals.
